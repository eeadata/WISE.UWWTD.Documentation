Reporting measures and costs#

Warning

Draft This page describes the proposed measure-first model, which replaces the investment and funding fields of the UWWTP and Agglomeration tables and the OtherInvestment table (OI-22 Measure-first model). Nothing here is decided.

Measures first#

The programme starts from measures. A measure is what the Member State plans to do to reach or maintain compliance, or to renew existing infrastructure. Each measure has a cost, and that cost is financed from one or more sources:

  • Measure (Measure): one row per measure, with its type, coverage, priority, status, dates and investment cost.

  • MeasureObject (MeasureObject): the plants or agglomerations the measure concerns, if they are listed.

  • MeasureFinancing (MeasureFinancing): where the money comes from, source by source, and how far each part is secured.

The UWWTP and Agglomeration tables keep only the state of each listed plant or agglomeration: status, expected load and capacity. This follows the structure of the Water Framework Directive 4th cycle, where a Measure table is the main table and costs are reported per measure.

Renewal of compliant infrastructure under Article 23(1)(c) is a measure like any other, with the measure type renewOrReplaceCollectingSystem or renewOrReplaceTreatmentPlant. It can list the plants or agglomerations, or be national.

Cost#

  • Investment cost only. investmentCost is capital expenditure. Operational and maintenance costs are not reported.

  • Unknown is a valid answer. If the cost is not yet known, set costStatus to unknown and leave investmentCost empty. A Member State that knows it must upgrade ten plants in the next ten years but not what it will cost can say so.

  • Estimated or known. Use estimated for estimates and known for costs from a project, tender or contract.

  • Each cost once. A cost belongs to one measure. Do not add the cost of a plant to the agglomeration it serves, or the reverse. The total investment of the programme is the sum of investmentCost over all measures.

Financing#

Each row of MeasureFinancing gives one source of financing for a measure, with its status and amount:

  • Several sources. Each source, and each EU fund, is its own row with its own amount, so it is clear how much is expected from which fund.

  • Secured or not. fundingStatus says whether the money is secured, applied for or only planned, or whether no source has been identified.

  • The gap. The financing gap of a measure is calculated, not reported: investmentCost minus the sum of the amounts with fundingStatus = secured.

  • Funding is not an investment. Financing amounts describe how a measure’s cost is covered. They are not added to the total investment.

Currency#

As in the Water Framework Directive, amounts are reported in millions: of euro by euro area Member States, and of national currency by the others. millionUnitsOfNationalCurrency gives the currency once per measure, and applies to investmentCost and to the financing amounts of that measure. A value below one million uses decimals with a full stop: 10 000 is 0.01.

Reported once#

Measures for urban wastewater treatment plants and collecting systems are intended to be reported once, in this dataflow. Reporters would then no longer report them again in the programme of measures under the Water Framework Directive. Each measure type corresponds to a WFD key type of measure and main pressure, derived centrally, so that these measures can still be used in assessments under that Directive (Correspondence with the Water Framework Directive). This depends on agreement with the Water Framework Directive reporting (OI-25 Reporting measures once).

Prioritisation#

Article 23(1)(b) requires a prioritisation of investments related to:

  • the size of the agglomeration; and

  • the level of environmental impact of discharges of untreated urban wastewater and the related risks for the environment or human health.

The Directive does not link priority to the timing of completion. In the proposed model each measure has its own prioritisation, High, Medium or Low, and prioritisationReference in MSSummary points to the national prioritisation methodology.

Important

The draft description adds that “‘High’ should be used for projects that are scheduled for earlier completion relative to others”. A proposal to remove this link is under consideration, because high-priority projects can be complex and take longer to complete (OI-05 Prioritisation).

Dates#

  • Each measure has its own expectedCompletionYear, and optionally expectedStartYear. For a measure at a treatment plant, the completion year is the year of expected compliance.

  • If the plants or agglomerations under one measure finish in different years, split the measure (OI-23 One type and one completion year per measure).

  • Whether the first programme should cover measures to 2045 or a shorter horizon is open (OI-04 Reference date and investment horizon of the first programme).

Projected loads and capacity#

  • expectedLoad in UWWTP is the planned load entering the plant at the expected date of compliance. expectedCapacity is the planned organic design capacity in p.e. Hydraulic capacity is not requested.

  • expectedLoad in Agglomeration is the expected generated load of the agglomeration at the expected date of compliance. expectedLoadCollected and expectedLoadIndividualSystems give the expected percentages collected and addressed through individual systems.

Articles 3(3) and 6(5) set out how the load of an agglomeration and the load entering a plant are calculated: from the maximum average weekly load during the year, excluding unusual weather situations. The draft does not say whether projected loads should be estimated on the same basis (OI-12 Projected loads and capacity).

Examples#

Examples works through six situations raised in the consultation, from filling in the tables to adding the money back up: several measures at one plant, a collecting system and individual systems in one agglomeration, several funds for one project, a cost not yet known, part of the money still to be found, and a national renewal programme.
