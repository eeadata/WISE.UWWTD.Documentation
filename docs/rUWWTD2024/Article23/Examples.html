(art23-examples)= # Examples

:::{warning} Draft The examples follow the proposed measure-first model ({ref}art23-oi-measure-model). The situations come from questions and objections raised by Member States in the May 2026 consultation. The names, figures and codes are invented. :::

Each example is shown twice: first from the reporter’s side, filling in the tables, then from the analyst’s side, adding the money back up. The last section puts all six together.

To keep the tables readable:

  • codes are shown without the country code that a real code starts with;
  • all names are in English, so measureNameLanguage is eng throughout, and all amounts are in millions of euro, so the currency is EUR;
  • optional fields that are left empty are not shown.

(art23-example-two-dates)= ## 1. Two projects at one plant, on different dates

The question. A Member State asked what to do when several investments are planned at one treatment plant, on different dates. The draft format allowed several measures per plant, but only one start date and one completion date.

Filling it in. Rivermouth plant must remove nitrogen and phosphorus by 2033, and micropollutants by 2040. These are two separate projects, with different designs, budgets and funders, seven years apart. They are reported as two measures, each with its own dates and cost. The nitrogen and phosphorus works are one construction contract with one budget, so they are one measure of type upgradeTertiaryNitrogenPhosphorus, and the cost is not split between the two nutrients.

Both measures list the same plant in euUWWTPCode, and the agglomeration it serves, AGG0007, in euAgglomerationCode, so that the investments can be found for that agglomeration, as Article 23(1)(b) requires. The status, projected load and capacity of the plant and the agglomeration are reported once, in the UWWTP and Agglomeration tables, not on either measure.

(art23-example-collecting-individual)= ## 2. A collecting system and individual systems in one agglomeration

The question. A Member State asked what happens when both the collecting system and individual systems are to be invested in, in the same agglomeration: should one total be given, or one row for each?

Filling it in. Northtown is extending its collecting system and, for the outlying houses, supporting individual systems. These are two measures, with their own dates and costs, both listing the agglomeration in euAgglomerationCode. Had they been one project, with one budget and one completion year, they could have been one measure with both types. The Agglomeration table gives the expected split of the load between the two.

(art23-example-several-funds)= ## 3. One project, several funds, several agglomerations

The question. A Member State pointed out that one fund name per row is not enough, because several funds may be requested for one project.

Filling it in. The Eastvale interconnection is one project that connects three agglomerations to a shared collecting system. It is one measure, with the three agglomerations in euAgglomerationCode. Its cost is given once, for the whole project, and not divided between the agglomerations. Each source of financing, including each EU fund, is its own row with its own amount.

(art23-example-cost-unknown)= ## 4. A cost that cannot be estimated yet

The question. Several Member States said that investment costs cannot be estimated before the planning, design or feasibility studies are done, and that forecasts for less advanced projects would be very uncertain.

Filling it in. The Member State knows that ten small coastal plants must remove nitrogen within the next ten years, but not yet which plants come first or what the work will cost. It reports one measure for plants not yet identified, gives their number, and says that the cost is unknown. There are no financing rows.

numberOfObjects is 10. Since the plants, and so their agglomerations, are not yet identified, the measure raises a warning (check R14): Article 23(1)(b) asks for the investments for each agglomeration, so the plants and agglomerations should be listed in the next update.

The unknown cost raises a second warning (check R16): Article 23(1)(b) asks for an indicative financial estimation, and this measure gives none. The delivery is accepted, but an estimate is preferred, even a rough one. Had the Member State used a typical cost of 1.5 million per plant, it would report costStatus = estimated and an investmentCost of 15.0, and no R16 warning.

(art23-example-gap)= ## 5. Part of the money still to be found

The question. The Commission wants to know whether funding has been secured, and what is still missing. For example: a measure costs 100, half of it is secured, and the Member State is applying for more.

Filling it in. The Lakeside upgrade concerns two plants, which both serve agglomeration AGG0301. It adds nutrient removal and quaternary treatment at both, under one contract with one budget and one completion year. It is therefore one measure with two types, upgradeTertiaryNitrogenPhosphorus and upgradeQuaternary, and its cost is given once, not split between them. Half of the cost is secured from the operators’ own funds, an application for the Cohesion Fund is pending, and the source of the rest is not yet identified.

The wrong answers, and why.

:::{list-table} Common mistakes :header-rows: 1 :widths: 40 20 40

    • Method
    • Result
    • What went wrong
    • Add up investmentCost after joining the measures to their plants and agglomerations
    • 866.4
    • Example 1 is counted twice, and examples 3 and 5 three times
    • Add up the costs and the financing amounts together
    • 1 212.8
    • Financing describes the same money as the cost
    • Add up investmentCost once per measure type
    • 706.4
    • Example 5 has two types and is counted twice
    • Treat the measure without a cost as zero
    • 606.4, presented as complete
    • Example 4 is missing from the total, and nothing says so :::

The rule. Add up costs per measure, then join to plants and agglomerations, or to measure types, only to see which ones a measure concerns, never to divide or repeat its cost. Report the measures without a cost next to every total, as a count. Keep secured financing apart from financing that is only applied for or planned, and keep loans apart from grants.
